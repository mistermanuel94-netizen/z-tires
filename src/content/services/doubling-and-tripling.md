## What is it?

Tripling means pressing three used tyres inside one another so they occupy the space of roughly one
and a half. Doubling does the same with two. FlexTyres offers both, and describes the effect
plainly: **"We will pack up to three used tyres in one. This way we are able to double the amount of
used tyres fitting into a container."**

That is why the published container capacities are quoted as *tripled* quantities — approximately
1,350 tyres in a 20 ft container and approximately 2,800 in a 40 ft container.

## Who is it for?

Any buyer paying for container space rather than for tyres alone. The further a shipment travels,
the more the packing method matters, because freight is charged by volume long before it is charged
by weight.

## The two methods FlexTyres publishes

### Tripling by machine

A high-end technique that packs three tyres inside each other. It reduces the space taken by the
packing, the cost of transportation, and — because fewer journeys move the same number of tyres —
the environmental impact of the transport itself.

### Tripling by hand

A simpler approach than the machine method. It also reduces the cost of transportation and, in
FlexTyres' words, "helps our environment thrive".

## What it costs

FlexTyres' published answer, from its own FAQ: *"For a little extra charge we double or triple the
tyres."* No fixed price is published — the charge forms part of the quotation for your order.

## How to arrange it

Say so when you request a quote. Container capacities on the car and truck tyre pages already assume
tripled packing, so if you need tyres shipped loose, tell us — the quantities will change.
